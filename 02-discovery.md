# Discovery

This is the area I had primary ownership of: framing the problem, motivation, and objectives that guided the rest of the team's work.

## Problem statement

Even with many online learning platforms available, existing systems don't adapt to the individual learner. The most critical gap is the lack of feedback systems that respond to a user's actual performance — most platforms don't use quiz or assessment data to adjust difficulty or guide the learner's next step. On top of that, dashboards on major platforms like edX are often cumbersome to navigate. Together, these gaps — weak personalization, no response to performance data, and clunky navigation — limit how much learners actually get out of these platforms.

## Motivation

Most online learners get a one-size-fits-all experience: the same content, same pace, same feedback (or lack of it), regardless of who they are or how they're doing. This ignores real differences in learning pace, style, and background knowledge, and tends to produce passive content consumption rather than active engagement. The motivation behind uiuX was to close that gap — using in-lecture quizzes, tiered learner levels, and note/time-tracking tools to turn passive watching into active, measured progress.

## Objectives

1. Replace passive video consumption with embedded interactivity — pop-up quizzes and discussion touchpoints during lectures.
2. Build an algorithm that classifies learners (Beginner / Average / Advanced) from quiz and activity performance, and personalizes their path accordingly.
3. Give learners a single dashboard to track courses completed, quizzes taken, and lectures watched.
4. Support retention with real-time note-taking, downloadable lectures, and transcripts.
5. Make the platform accessible across devices, with accessibility support (screen readers, subtitles).

## Survey takeaways

A user survey run alongside this research found strong interest in real-time quizzes and personalized feedback, high value placed on milestone tracking with improvement plans, and demand for downloadable lectures/transcripts for accessibility. These findings validated the direction set by the objectives above.
